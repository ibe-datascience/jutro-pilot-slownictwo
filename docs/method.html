Method

What each step is for, and which methods, packages and models it uses

Thresholds and model names on this page are read from the configuration of the run.

Models

Task Model Step
Label every candidate, twice and independently Sonnet 5.5, Opus 5.5 5
Settle disagreements and low-confidence cases Fable 5.2 5
Group terms into concepts Fable 5.2 5
List terms directly from the materials (check on step 4) Opus 5.5 5
Generate pseudowords Opus 5.5 7
Rate pseudowords Fable 5.2 7
Lemmatise, as two of the tools compared in the benchmark Haiku 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 2

All requests go through the Claude Batch API (anthropic 2.8.0) with JSON-schema output.

1 · Corpus

Why: subject words stand out only against the text of other subjects.

What How Tool
Target materials of the subject Biologia, school stage E2 ZPE dump
Contrast materials of all other subjects of the same stage, without foreign languages; six per target material in a sample run ZPE dump
Text HTML parsed into titles, body, definitions, exercises, captions lxml 6.1.3
Cleaning sentences repeated in at least 25 materials, interface instructions and licence lines are removed own rules

2 · Tagging and lemmatisation

Why: Polish inflects, so counting words needs their base forms.

What How Tool
Lemma and part of speech spaCy tags each token; the Morfeusz2 reading that agrees with spaCy gives the lemma spacy 3.8.18 (pl_core_news_lg), morfeusz2 1.99.15
Check 300 forms, mostly rare terms and phrases; spaCy and Stanza, each with and without Morfeusz2, Morfeusz2 alone, Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 stanza 1.15.0

3 · Frequencies

Why: general frequency tells a rare term from an everyday word, and it is what matching uses later.

What How Resource
Used in the lists KWJP100 lemma frequencies, on the Zipf scale KWJP100 (CC BY 4.0)
Compared only coverage of the biology vocabulary and agreement with KWJP100 NKJP, SUBTLEX-PL, wordfreq 3.1.1

4 · Candidates

Why: a complete and repeatable list of everything that could be a term, made before any model is involved.

What How Tool
Single words lemmas of content words known to Morfeusz2; proper names dropped own code
Phrases noun phrases of 2–4 words found by syntactic patterns (adjective + noun, noun + genitive), rebuilt in the nominative own code (frazy.py)
Subject candidates at least 3 occurrences in at least 2 biology materials, or at least 5 in one
General candidates at least 5 occurrences across at least 3 other subjects
Scores log-likelihood and log ratio against the contrast corpus and against KWJP100, Simple Maths, TF-IDF across subjects, PMI and logDice for phrases, C-value, rank fusion of all of them own code; C-value from TermoPL 8.1.1

The scores rank candidates and are compared with each other on the Results page. They do not decide the labels: all subject candidates (up to 4000) and a stratified sample of 1500 general candidates go to the models.

5 · Labels

Why: statistics cannot tell a biology term from a school word that happens to be frequent in biology.

What How
The model sees the candidate, its counts in biology and in the other subjects, up to three sentences from the corpus
The model returns a label, a corrected base form if needed, whether the term can be shown in a picture, its confidence, a one-sentence reason
Labels T3 biology term · T3_INNY term of another subject · T2 school vocabulary · T1 everyday word · SZUM not usable
Final label the label of Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5 if they agree and are confident; otherwise Fable 5.2 votes too and the majority of three decides; no majority → marked for review
Concepts Fable 5.2 groups variants, synonyms and derivatives of one concept, in one request over all T3 terms

6 · Pools

Why: the test compares terms with general words, so the two sets must not differ in frequency or length.

What How Tool
Subject pool all candidates labelled T3
General pool all candidates labelled T1 or T2
Matching one general word per term: same part of speech, closest Zipf frequency and length; optimal assignment without reuse, within 1 Zipf and 3 letters scipy 1.18.1 (linear_sum_assignment)
Worksheet 400 candidates, stratified by label, for two annotators; model labels are not shown openpyxl 3.1.5

7 · Pseudowords

Why: the word-recognition test needs non-words that look and sound Polish and cannot be spotted by their shape.

Each pseudoword is tied to a template: one of 240 real nouns and verbs drawn from the two pools. Each generator gives up to 12 candidates per template.

Generator How Tool or model
wuggy_koncowka replaces sub-syllabic segments of the template, keeps their lengths and the template’s ending Wuggy 1.1.2, Polish plugin
wuggy the same, ending free Wuggy 1.1.2
unipseudo letter-trigram chain built from real words of the same length and part of speech UniPseudo (Python port), WorldLex-Polish
llm the model is given the template and asked for non-words with the same ending and number of syllables Opus 5.5
imbir published list, items accepted by at least 4 of 5 judges, paired by length Imbir et al. (2015)

Hard filters; a candidate is rejected by the first one it fails:

Filter Rule
Existing word found in Morfeusz2, KWJP100, WorldLex, wordfreq or the corpus
Shape length within one letter of the template, same number of syllables, same kind of ending
Letter patterns every trigram, onset, coda and consonant cluster occurs in at least 5 of 64 293 Polish lemmas
Too close to a word one edit from the template or from a frequent word (Zipf ≥ 3), or sounds like a word
Transparent a real stem with a prefix or suffix, or two words joined
Unsuitable vulgar association; sounds like one of 54 295 frequent Ukrainian or Russian words

Edit distances: rapidfuzz 3.14.6.

What How
Ranking within a template, by closeness to it in neighbourhood density (OLD20) and bigram probability
Rating Fable 5.2 rates the top 6 per template, at most half from one generator: pronounceability 1–5, similarity to a real word 0–3, and flags for existing, foreign and inappropriate
Recommended list pronounceability ≥ 4, similarity ≤ 1, no flag; the best-ranked 2 per template
Check templates and pseudowords compared on length, syllables, clusters, diacritics, OLD20, bigram probability
