1korpus Build the corpus
  • Select catalogue entries of school stage E2: materials of the target subject (Biologia) and, as contrast, of all other subjects. Teacher-facing material types and foreign-language subjects are dropped.
  • With --probka N: draw N target and 6·N contrast materials (seeded).
  • Parse the HTML into typed segments: title, body, definition, exercise, caption, image description.
  • Remove sentences repeated across many materials (platform templates), interface instructions and licence or metadata lines.
  • Estimate the grade of each target material from topic keywords.
  • Collect glossary entries and the editors' pairs inflected form → base form as silver standards.

2tagowanie Tag and lemmatise; compare tools
  • Whole corpus: spaCy tokenises and tags; for each token the Morfeusz2 reading that agrees with spaCy's part of speech, case, number and gender gives the lemma and inflectional class.
  • Target corpus again with Stanza, for comparison; speed test of the three tools on the same text.
  • Benchmark on 300 forms (150 rare terms, 100 multiword expressions, 50 others): spaCy and Stanza, each alone and with Morfeusz2, Morfeusz2 first reading, Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5.
  • Reference: the editors' base form where ZPE has one, otherwise a provisional answer from Opus 5.5; the sheet has columns for a human checker.

4kandydaci Extract and score candidates
  • Single-word candidates: lemmas of content words known to Morfeusz2; lemmas tagged mostly as proper names are dropped.
  • Multiword candidates: noun phrases of 2–4 words found within a sentence by syntactic patterns (agreeing adjectives, genitive modifiers), rebuilt in the nominative; phrases built on pronouns, determiners and instruction words are skipped.
  • Subject candidates: at least 3 occurrences in at least 2 target materials, or at least 5 in a single one. General candidates: words spread over at least 3 other subjects.
  • Score every candidate with every method, using the true corpus sizes: log-likelihood and log ratio against the contrast corpus; log-likelihood, log ratio and Simple Maths against KWJP100; TF-IDF across subjects; PMI and logDice of the weakest adjacent pair for phrases; TermoPL C-value; reciprocal-rank fusion of the rankings.
  • Compare each ranking with the silver standards (precision and recall at K, against a random baseline) and count how many silver entries are reachable at all.
  • Keep up to three real sentences per candidate, from different materials, for the models and the annotators.

5llm Classify candidates with language models
  • Send all subject candidates (up to 4000) and a sample of 1500 general candidates stratified by general frequency and number of words.
  • Prompt per candidate: the form, its counts in biology and in the other subjects, and up to three sentences. The rule-based pre-label is not shown.
  • Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5 independently return a category (T3 biology term, T3_INNY term of another subject, T2 school vocabulary, T1 everyday word, SZUM not usable), the kind of noise, a corrected base form, imageability, confidence and a one-sentence reason.
  • Where the two labels differ or either confidence is low, Fable 5.2 labels the candidate too; the majority of the three decides, and cases without a majority are marked for review.
  • Fable 5.2 groups all T3 terms into concepts in a single request.
  • Separately, Opus 5.5 lists terms directly from 30 materials, as a check on what step 4 missed.
  • The cost of each batch is estimated before it is sent; the run stops rather than pass the limit of 100 USD. An interrupted batch is collected on the next run, not sent again.

6pule Build the pools and the annotation worksheet
  • Join statistics with the final labels. Only labelled candidates enter the pools: T3 the subject pool, T1 and T2 the general pool.
  • A corrected base form replaces the extracted one in forma_podstawowa if it keeps the number of words; if two rows then name the same item, the more frequent one is kept.
  • Match each subject term to one general item of the same part of speech and number of words, closest in Zipf frequency and length in letters: optimal assignment without reuse, within calipers.
  • Report balance of frequency and length before and after matching.
  • Attach the concept of each subject term.
  • Draw 400 candidates stratified by final label and by whether the case was adjudicated into a worksheet for two human annotators, with sentences and without model labels; keep the key and the sampling weights aside. If a filled-in worksheet is present, score the labels against it.

7pseudoslowa Generate, filter and rate pseudowords
  • Templates: 240 real nouns and verbs in base form, 35% from the subject pool and the rest from the general pool.
  • Up to 12 candidates per template from each generator: Wuggy keeping the template's ending, Wuggy with a free ending, UniPseudo, a language model, and the Imbir list.
  • Reference lexicon: KWJP100 lemmas known to Morfeusz2, with counts of letter trigrams, onsets, codas and clusters.
  • Hard filters, each rejection with its reason: existing word; length, syllables or ending unlike the template; rare letter pattern; one edit from a frequent word; homophone of a word; existing stem with an affix or a compound; vulgar association; Ukrainian or Russian word in Polish spelling.
  • Measure what passes: OLD20 and neighbours over the whole lexicon, bigram probability, the same for the templates.
  • The model rates up to 6 per template for pronounceability, associations and foreign or inappropriate readings; the best 2 rated positively are recommended.
  • Compare the distributions of templates and pseudowords (standardised differences).
